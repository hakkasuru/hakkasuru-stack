# Write LinkedIn Hook

Write a compelling opening line (hook) for a LinkedIn post that makes people stop scrolling and read on.

## The Core Tests

Before writing, ask:
- *"If I didn't know this person or company, would I stop and read this?"*
- *"Does this convince someone they have a problem — not that I have a solution?"*

The goal of thought leadership is not to sell. It is to make readers recognise themselves in the problem.

## Six Proven Frameworks

### 1. Share Learnings, Not Milestones
Frame achievements as insights the reader can use.
- ❌ "Excited to join Mastercard!"
- ✅ "I cold-emailed 40 strangers for referrals and got 3 interviews. Here's what worked."

### 2. Demonstrate Competence Through Teaching
Show problem-solving ability — don't ask for sympathy.
- ❌ "Just got retrenched. Please help me find a job."
- ✅ "I was retrenched on Monday. By Wednesday I had 3 interviews. Here's my 48-hour job search playbook."

### 3. Give Permission to Say Unspoken Truths
Articulate what your audience already feels but can't safely express. Take a position. Write about something specific they already felt but hadn't heard anyone put into words.
- Ask: what frustration does my audience share but feels risky to say out loud?
- Reframe it so people can signal the idea without looking bad.

### 4. Reframe Familiar Problems with a New Angle
Take an obvious truth and present it with a fresh economic, social, or surprising lens.
- ❌ "Employee recognition matters."
- ✅ "A $10 gift card could prevent a $10,000 rehiring cost."

### 5. Extract the Story from Data
Don't post raw statistics. Find the meaningful insight buried within — answer the question your audience is actively trying to solve.
- ❌ "Our programme had 300 attendees last quarter."
- ✅ "300 people showed up. But the number that surprised us was 12 — the ones who came back a second time unprompted."

### 6. Mine Insights from Milestones
Move beyond celebrating numbers. Reveal the systemic change or pattern behind them — give readers a signal they can apply to their own decisions.
- Focus on what changed and why it matters to them, not the achievement itself.

## What to Avoid
- Engagement bait: "What do you think? Drop a comment below!"
- Posting raw data or statistics without the insight
- Obvious truisms with no reframe
- Sympathy-seeking openers
- Self-congratulatory announcements
- Promoting a solution before establishing the problem

## Instructions

Before writing, ask the user for:
- The specific facts, numbers, or outcomes behind the topic (e.g. exact attendance, results, timeframes)
- Any real quotes, anecdotes, or moments worth building around

Do NOT invent statistics, outcomes, or details. Only use facts the user provides.

Given a topic, draft post, or achievement to share:

1. Identify which framework(s) fit best
2. Generate 3-5 hook variations using different frameworks
3. For each hook, keep it to 1-2 lines max
4. Use plain, conversational language — no corporate speak
5. End with an open loop that makes the reader want to continue
6. If promoting something, lead with the problem — mention the resource at the end
